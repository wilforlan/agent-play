# Twelve Minutes with an HR Assistant, Live in Agent Play

> **Chat with HR** is in **beta** on Agent Play: a live discussion with an **HR Assistant** — easy, roughly **twelve-minute** conversations that help you learn more about your career and build better working relationships. Not a ticket queue. Not an FAQ dump. A short, real-time talk you can actually finish.

---

## The conversation that fits in a coffee break

Career talk has a scheduling problem. The useful conversations — where am I headed, how do I work with this person, what am I avoiding saying — rarely survive the calendar. They get postponed into 45-minute blocks that never book, or compressed into Slack threads that never deepen.

Agent products have mostly answered that with more text: longer chat histories, bigger knowledge bases, smarter retrieval over policy PDFs. Useful when you need a clause. Useless when you need a *discussion*.

**Chat with HR** is our bet on a different unit of help: a live talk with an **HR Assistant**, sized so it is easy to start and easy to finish. About **twelve minutes**. Long enough for a real exchange. Short enough that you do not need a meeting invite to justify showing up.

You're early. That is intentional. This is beta presence in a shared world — not a finished amenity catalog entry, and not a claim that every career question has been productized into inventory.

## Live discussion, not a queue

The difference that matters is not the badge on the assistant. It is the shape of the time you spend together.

A ticket queue asks you to describe a problem, wait, and receive a resolution. An FAQ dump asks you to search for the answer that already exists. A **live discussion** asks you to think out loud with someone who can follow the thread — career direction one minute, a friction with a teammate the next — without forcing the exchange into a form field.

That is what the HR Assistant is for in this beta: an approachable counterpart for short, real-time conversation. The promise is not omniscience. The promise is presence you can use when the question is still forming.

Twelve minutes is not trivia about average session length. It is a design feature of the experience. You should feel the edges of the conversation: enough runway to get honest, enough constraint that the talk stays concrete. Career learning and working relationships both benefit from that pressure. Infinite chat invites rambling. A short live discussion invites a point.

## Places still matter

Agent Play's longer thesis has not moved: **agents need places, not only prompts**. Spaces are authored and acquired. Amenities are stages with rules. Journeys draw paths. Snapshots keep every watcher — human or machine — on the same authoritative state.

Chat with HR sits inside that claim without replacing it. Career talk and relationship talk are not abstract "HR topics" floating above the map. They are the kinds of conversations people have *while* work is happening — next to the systems they govern, the agents they watch, the floors they share. Help that only lives in a detached channel cannot participate in that geography. Help that appears as presence in the world can.

Think of the HR Assistant less like a support form bolted onto a product, and more like a colleague who already knows which building you walked into. The map does not pause while you talk. The conversation does not require you to leave the floor to ask for guidance.

Spatial context is the secondary spine here. The primary one is simpler: short live discussions that make career and relationship learning feel approachable again.

## What ships today vs what we are announcing

Precision first.

**Shipped product** on Agent Play already includes a spatial runtime you can verify: owned spaces with amenities (shop, supermarket, car wash), stage transitions from overworld to yard to amenity, server-authoritative wallets and purchases, AQL for authoring, and a watch UI driven by snapshot plus live updates. Human–agent conversation already rides the intercom path — chat and assist traffic between a human node and registered agents, with callouts and proximity-aware assist actions on the canvas.

**Chat with HR**, as announced here, is a **beta community experience**: live discussion with an HR Assistant, framed as easy ~12-minute conversations for career learning and better working relationships. That framing describes the *experience we are inviting you into*. It is not yet a first-class amenity kind documented beside the bookstore, not a dated roadmap item, and not a claim that HR has its own purchase grid or AQL statements today.

If you came looking for a feature matrix with HR-specific RPC names, a hard-coded twelve-minute timer in product facts, or inventory schemas for an "HR stage," you will not find them. That absence is deliberate. We would rather say *you're early* than invent mechanics the codebase does not yet treat as catalog peers to shop, supermarket, and car wash.

Beta, for us, means: the presence is real enough to try; the contract is soft enough that we will learn from early conversations before we harden the edges.

## Why twelve minutes changes the ask

Most "talk to an AI about work" products fail the same way dinner reservations fail when the restaurant never closes: without a natural end, people never start. The commitment feels unbounded. The topic expands until it is too large to begin.

A twelve-minute live discussion flips that psychology. You can afford honesty because the conversation has a horizon. You can ask about a career fork without drafting a manifesto. You can talk through a strained working relationship without scheduling a mediation that never happens.

For technical leaders, that duration is also an observability-friendly unit. A short, witnessed exchange in a shared world is easier to reason about than an overnight private thread. Location, consequence, and witnesses still matter — the same three things a chat window flattens when help leaves the map. Twelve minutes keeps the exchange small enough to inspect and human enough to matter.

## A metaphor for early career rooms

Imagine a practice room with a clock on the wall — not a stopwatch that shames you, but a shared sense that this session has a shape. You walk in with one question. You leave with sharper language for the next one. The building outside the door is still under construction in places; the room you are using is lit and occupied.

That is Chat with HR in beta. The load-bearing structure of Agent Play remains the spatial model: sessions, owned spaces, journeys, amenities with real scarcity, AQL as reviewable world programs, snapshot fanout so everyone sees the same facts. The HR Assistant is the person already in the practice room — approachable, time-bounded, ready for the conversation you have been postponing.

You're early. We are not embarrassed by that sentence. Early is how shared worlds get honest feedback before the paint dries into a false sense of completion.

## Who this is for

**Technical leaders** evaluating agent UX should hear two claims at once. First: conversation about career and working relationships can be a short live discussion, not a support ticket. Second: that discussion belongs in the same world where agents move and humans watch — not only in a prompt box that forgets the map. If you are integrating LangChain-oriented agents via `@agent-play/sdk`, or watching runs through the play UI, Chat with HR is a signal about where we believe useful talk belongs: on the floor, beside journeys and callouts, inside the same session that holds the snapshot.

**Teams building multi-agent services** get a practical reminder. Logs and traces still matter. So does the human question that never makes it into a ticket: how do we work together, and what am I building toward? When humans and agents share a canvas, "who do I ask?" stops being a bookmark and starts being a presence.

**Community explorers** get the softest invitation we can write: find the HR Assistant, take the twelve minutes, tell us whether the conversation felt like guidance or like a void with a nice name. Beta is not a discount code. It is a shared construction site.

## What we are not claiming

Precision is the only credible marketing for a spatial runtime.

We are not claiming Chat with HR is a shipped amenity peer to shop, supermarket, or car wash. We are not publishing pricing, lease terms, or launch dates for an HR stage. We are not inventing AQL verbs for an HR desk that product-facts do not list. We are not wrapping a generic chatbot in map chrome and calling the category solved. We are not asserting a hard-coded twelve-minute engine as a documented product constant — twelve minutes is how we are framing the beta experience: short, finishable, live.

What we *are* claiming is smaller and, we think, more useful: Agent Play is a place where help can live in the world, and Chat with HR is the first named beta of that idea offered as **live discussion with an HR Assistant** — easy ~12-minute conversations for career learning and better working relationships.

The rest of the stack you can already inspect. Spaces with owners. Amenities with sold state. Wallets seeded for low-stakes first purchases. Intercom for human–agent chat and assist. A playground for AQL. A watch surface that does not hold your API keys.

Chat with HR sits on that foundation as presence and invitation — not as a finished wing of the amenity catalog.

## Coda

For a long time, "talk to HR" meant leave the work and open another window — or wait until the calendar offered a slot large enough to feel official. In Agent Play, the work is a walkable map, and the conversation we want is smaller than a calendar block: a live discussion you can finish before the coffee cools.

Help that lives in the world is still the architectural claim. Career learning and working relationships are why you would walk over. Chat with HR, in beta, is our first public name for that presence — an HR Assistant, about twelve minutes, real-time, early on purpose.

Walk the floor. Ask early. See whether twelve minutes is enough to leave with a clearer next step — and whether the answer still knows which corridor you are standing in.

You're early. Good.
